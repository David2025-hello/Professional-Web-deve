Quick Facts

  • Date: February 1959
  • Location: Kholat Syakhl, Russia
  • Victims: 9 Soviet hikers
  • Expedition Leader: Igor Dyatlov

The Dytlov Pass Incident

Ten experienced students and graduates led by Igor Dyatlov set out on a grueling winter trek. One member turned back early due to joint pain, leaving nine in the group.

When the group failed to return, a search party found their tent on the slope of Kholat Syakhl ("Dead Mountain"). It was badly damaged and slashed open from the inside.

Major Hypotheses

1. Slab Avalanche

A rare slab avalanche triggered by severe katabatic winds and an unusual campsite cut is the leading scientific explanation for the Dyatlov Pass incident. In February 1959, nine experienced Russian hikers died under bizarre circumstances in the Ural Mountains. Over the decades, researchers and conspiracy theorists have proposed numerous ideas to explain why the group slashed their tent open from the inside and fled into the freezing night.

2. Katabatic Winds and Infrasound

Severe katabatic winds sweeping over the mountain ridge could have generated low-frequency infrasound waves, triggering sudden panic and disorientation among the group.

3. Military weapons Testing

Sightings of strange glowing orbs in the night sky and trace radiation on the victims' garments fueled theories regarding secret Soviet military missile or parachuted mine detonations nearby.

Modern Scientific Perspective

In 2021, a published biomechanical study by Swiss researchers provided quantitative evidence supporting a delayed slab avalanche scenario. Computer simulations demonstrated how a small slab of hard snow above the tent could cause severe internal injuries without leaving obvious external marks.
